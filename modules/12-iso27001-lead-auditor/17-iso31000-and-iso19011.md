# ISO 31000 and ISO 19011

> Module 12, lesson 17. _The general-purpose risk and audit standards that sit
> behind ISO 27001 — guidance, not requirements._

## Summary

Two ISO standards that aren't part of ISO 27001 but turn up around it. Both are
**guidance**, both apply well beyond IT, and **neither is certifiable** — no
organisation can be "ISO 31000 certified". They rarely come up in day-to-day
consulting; the place you'll see them is a line in an ISO 27001 report or appendix
saying they were used as guidance. Monash University's published summary, read
earlier in this module, is an example: its ISMS is "supported by ISO 31000 aligned
risk management practices".

Neither is mandatory for ISO 27001 certification. The reason to know them is to
recognise them — and because an interviewer may ask.

## ISO 31000:2018 — risk management

Principles and guidelines for managing any kind of risk — strategic, operational,
financial, security — in any organisation.

**Eight principles.** Risk management should be integrated into organisational
processes; structured and comprehensive; customised to the context; inclusive of
stakeholders; dynamic; based on the best available information; considerate of
human and cultural factors; and continually improved.

**Three components:** the *principles*; the *framework* (leadership, structure,
integration into the organisation); and the *process*.

**The process:**

| Activity | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| Communication and consultation | Engage stakeholders throughout |
| Scope, context and criteria | Define what's being assessed and against what |
| Risk assessment | Identify, analyse and evaluate |
| Risk treatment | Select and implement treatment |
| Monitoring and review | Track whether treatment works, adapt |
| Recording and reporting | Document and communicate results |

Most of this is already in the Stark ISMS. Context is clause 4; stakeholder
involvement ran through the risk and SoA work; the risk assessment and treatment
are clause 6; monitoring is clause 9. ISO 27001 was designed with these principles
in mind.

## ISO 19011:2018 — auditing management systems

Guidelines for anyone planning, managing or conducting management system audits,
internal or external — including how to evaluate auditor competence.

**Seven principles of auditing:** integrity; fair presentation (report
truthfully); due professional care; confidentiality; independence;
evidence-based approach; and risk-based approach — putting audit effort where risk
is highest, which was new in the 2018 edition.

**The audit process:** initiate the audit (scope, objectives, criteria); prepare
(documents, roles, audit plan); conduct (interviews, records, observation,
evidence); prepare and distribute the report; complete the audit; follow up on
corrective actions.

**Auditor competence:** knowledge of management systems and audit principles,
technical knowledge of the area audited, communication and interviewing skills,
and understanding of the standard in question. Auditors are assigned on competence
and impartiality — which is exactly what the clause 9.2 programme said.

## Worth adding

**ISO 31000 has an information-security sibling: ISO/IEC 27005.** It's the
security-specific risk management standard, built to support ISO 27001 and
consistent with 31000's approach. Organisations use either as guidance. 31000 is
broader and better known to risk teams outside IT; 27005 is narrower and closer to
the ISMS.

**Two activities in the 31000 process aren't steps.** Communication and
consultation, and monitoring and review, run *alongside* the whole process rather
than at one point in it. The standard draws them as continuous bands around the
assessment and treatment cycle. Numbering them "step 1" and "step 5" suggests
stakeholders are consulted once at the start and monitoring happens once near the
end, which isn't the idea.

**19011 isn't what certification bodies are held to.** It's guidance, mainly for
first-party (internal) and second-party (supplier) audits. Certification bodies
are bound by requirements standards instead: **ISO/IEC 17021-1** for any
management system certification, **ISO/IEC 27006** specifically for bodies
certifying ISO 27001, and **ISO/IEC 27007** as the ISMS-specific auditing
guidance built on 19011. 17021-1 is also where the rule I noted in clause 9 comes
from — a certification body can't certify an organisation it consulted for.

**A small correction on the audit steps:** in 19011, the closing meeting is part
of *conducting* the audit, not *completing* it. The audit is complete once the
planned activities are done and the report is distributed.

## My take

**Neither standard told me anything new, and that's the point.** Every principle in
both lists already appears somewhere in this course — context and stakeholders in
the risk module, independence and evidence in the audit module, the three lines of
defence. These standards are where ISO wrote down the general version; 27001 is
the security-specific application of it.

**The useful thing to know is the family structure.** 31000 and 19011 are generic
guidance; 27005 and 27007 are their security-specific counterparts; 17021-1 and
27006 are the rules certification bodies follow. Being able to place a standard in
that structure — guidance or requirement, generic or security-specific,
organisation or certifier — is more useful in an interview than knowing any one
of them in detail.
