# Lesson 21 — Becoming an ISO/IEC 27001 Lead Auditor

## Summary

This lesson opens the lead auditor section. A lead auditor (ISO 19011 calls the role *audit team leader*) runs an audit from preparation to reporting. That means coordinating the team, assigning work, handling communication with the auditee, tracking progress, reviewing findings, settling disagreements and leading the team to the audit conclusion.

Becoming one takes three layers of skill:

1. **Auditor competence.** Apply the audit principles, plan the audit, collect and verify evidence, interview people, sample, evaluate evidence against the criteria, write findings, protect confidentiality, and report.
2. **Leadership.** Plan, make decisions, communicate, resolve conflict, and manage the team's work.
3. **ISMS knowledge.** Know every 27001 clause, the required documents and records, risk assessment and treatment, the SoA, the link from risks to controls, Annex A, measurement, corrective action, and the difference between conformity and legal compliance.

The next lessons cover audit principles, managing an audit programme, conducting an audit, and audit team members.

## Key concepts

| | ISO/IEC 27001 | ISO 19011 |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Requirements ("shall"), certifiable | Guidance ("should"), not certifiable |
| Subject | The ISMS | How to audit any management system |
| Lead auditor uses it to | Set the audit criteria | Decide how the audit is run |

**Conformity vs compliance.** Conformity means meeting the requirements of the standard. Compliance means meeting the law, regulations and contracts. An ISMS can conform to 27001 and still breach GDPR, because 27001 only requires the organisation to *identify* its legal requirements (4.2, A.5.31) and manage them. The certificate does not attest that the organisation is legally compliant.

## Where I'd push back

- **Check the edition before citing "ISO 19011:2026".** My lesson 17 notes cite ISO 19011:2018. A revision has been in progress. If it has been published, the lesson 17 note should be updated. If it hasn't, the citation should stay 2018. I won't change the notes until I've confirmed the edition on iso.org.
- **"ISO 19011 provides the guidance needed to lead audits" holds for internal and supplier audits.** For certification audits, the binding rules are ISO/IEC 17021-1 for certification bodies and ISO/IEC 27006-1 / 27007 for ISMS-specific competence and audit practice. A certification body lead auditor works to those rules, with 19011 in the background.
- **Being a "lead auditor" is a role, not something the standard grants.** Course certificates show training. Certification bodies decide competence through their own evaluation, including witnessed audits.

## My take

The useful idea here is the split between criteria and method: 27001 tells me *what* to check, and 19011 tells me *how* to check it. If I keep that straight, findings stay tied to a specific "shall" and don't drift into my own preferences.
