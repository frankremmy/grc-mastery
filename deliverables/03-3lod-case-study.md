## Why this one is worth reading carefully

Foresight is an FCA-regulated investment manager, so its risk disclosure is
written to a regulatory standard rather than for marketing. It's a real example of
everything module 2 and 3 have covered — a 3LOD structure, a risk register owned
by a named function, risk appetite with thresholds, a top-ten risk table with
causes, consequences and controls, and cyber risk treated as a principal risk with
board-level oversight.

## The governance structure

Bodies named in the report and what each is said to do:

| Body | Role per the report |
| --- | --- |
| **Board of Foresight Group Holdings Limited** | Accountable for the overall adequacy and effectiveness of the Group-wide risk framework and risk culture. Delegates responsibility for risk management to the Executive Committee |
| **Executive Committee (Foresight Group)** | Responsible for overall adequacy and effectiveness of the Group-wide risk framework; approves risk policies, procedures, roles and responsibilities; sets "tone from the top"; establishes risk appetite and strategy; monitors risk reporting and ensures mitigating action |
| **Members Board (Prudential Consolidation Group — Foresight Group LLP and subsidiaries)** | Same responsibilities at Firm level; stewardship of "tone from the top" |
| **Firm Risk Committee** | Periodic review of integrity and adequacy of risk reporting; independent review of adherence to risk policies and procedures; recommends and verifies implementation of process and control improvements; tracks and reports incidents, losses and breaches |
| **Audit & Risk Committee** | Receives **quarterly** reports on the risk profile of the UK, Luxembourg and Australian entities from the Head of Risk for Foresight Group LLP. Principal risks are discussed here and the assessment and control framework is challenged |
| **Risk Committee** | Convenes **monthly**; debates the evolving threat landscape. Cyber risk is a **standing agenda item** |
| **IT Steering Committee** | Together with the Risk Committee, provides oversight of cyber risk management |
| **Sustainability Committee** | Head of Risk is a member; oversight of sustainability risk management |

Reporting lines worth noting: the regional risk officers (Luxembourg and
Australia) report to the Head of Risk and the Chief Risk Officer.

## The three lines, as Foresight defines them

The report states plainly that the Group operates a 3LOD model **with risk
management oversight owned by and managed within the second line**.

| Line | Who | Responsibilities per the report |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **First** | Businesses and functions | Primary responsibility for an adequate control environment and adherence to risk policies and procedures; primary responsibility for managing investment risk in line with fund mandates; input into risk identification and scoring; notification and escalation of losses and incidents; provision of data for risk reporting |
| **Second** | Risk & Compliance | Day-to-day implementation of the risk framework; risk identification, scoring and prioritisation with input from first line; preparation of risk reporting; implements and monitors risk policies and procedures; advises on risk impact of regulatory and other external change; loss and incident analysis; monitoring of investment limits. Owns the **risk taxonomy**, the **Group risk register**, and the **management of risk events** (recording, escalation, reporting) through the Risk Committee |
| **Third** | **External oversight / assurance** | Verification of the adequacy of the Group risk framework; non-executive challenge of the overall adequacy and effectiveness of actions taken by the Risk Committee |

**The detail most likely to be asked about:** Foresight's third line is labelled
*external oversight/assurance* rather than an in-house internal audit department.
That differs from the textbook model, where third line is internal audit. It's a
legitimate arrangement — what matters is independence from the first and second
lines — and it contrasts directly with the Oscorp scenario, where an internal
audit function exists but covers only financial audit.

## RCSA — Risk and Control Self-Assessment

The Group's core risk identification process, and a first-line activity:

- Inherent risks are identified and assessed by **first line** resources, alongside
  the controls and their proven or expected mitigating effects.
- Supported by the risk functions across the Group.
- Risk is aggregated across businesses, themes and functions as directed by the
  Executive Committee, producing Group risk reporting and the qualitative and
  quantitative basis for risk appetite.
- The Risk team meets heads and risk owners of businesses and core functions
  **monthly** to assess existing, emerging and evolving risks.
- Migration underway from former RCSA processes onto an enterprise risk
  management platform, moving toward more regular assessment embedded in
  day-to-day activity.

Note the shape: it is a *self*-assessment by first line, supported and then
challenged by second line. That's the module 3 lesson-2 point about first-line
self-reporting being useful but insufficient on its own — here it's explicitly
the input to a second-line process rather than the final word.

## Risk appetite

- Required as a provider of regulated services. Foresight Group LLP (principal
  office, London) sets the **minimum standard for the Group**.
- The Risk Appetite Statement covers the aggregate level and types of risk the
  Group will accept or avoid, with qualitative statements and quantitative
  measures (earnings, capital, concentration, liquidity and others).
- **Staggered risk limits**: Early Warning Indicators ("EWIs") and hard limits.
- Categories covered: business and strategic risk, market risk, credit risk,
  operational risk, legal and regulatory risk, financial crime risk, conduct risk,
  and **information security risk**.
- Risk position versus appetite is reviewed **annually**; metric changes are
  reviewed, challenged and adopted by the Board.

The two-tier limit structure is worth remembering — an EWI fires before the hard
limit is breached, which is what makes appetite operational rather than
aspirational.

## Cyber and information security in the report

### How it's managed

- The **Technology and Data team** maintains the secure perimeter and detects and
  responds to threats in real time.
- Senior management including the Executive Committee are actively engaged and
  regularly updated on threats, mitigations, and business continuity and response
  plans.
- Oversight sits with the **IT Steering Committee and the Risk Committee**; cyber
  is a standing Risk Committee agenda item.
- Cyber defences are tested regularly through **simulated attacks (penetration
  testing)**, feeding a programme of work to enhance the digital estate.
- The Board receives updates on the emerging and evolving cyber threat landscape.

### Third-party cyber risk

- Explicit concern that attackers might reach the Group through a **compromised
  third party**.
- A **detailed IT security assessment** forms part of due diligence on providers.
- Its outcome feeds the **Third-Party Risk Assessment** and is a significant factor
  in deciding whether to proceed with or retain the relationship.
- Separately, counterparties are assessed annually for importance to operations,
  with the most important subject to **annual on-site due diligence**.

### The phishing and AI point

- AI tools have significantly improved phishing quality, making attacks harder to
  recognise.
- Security awareness training covers the latest phishing techniques.
- **Regular test emails** identify which staff need additional training.
- An internal review and escalation programme was implemented to align training
  and event response with the risk strategy.

### Cyber risk in the top ten

Listed as a **principal risk**: "Cyber risk: information security" — staff and
asset managers vulnerable to data breaches from cyberattacks, with financial
losses, regulatory fines and reputational damage.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Causes** | Phishing attacks; malware and ransomware; insider threats; unpatched software |
| **Consequences** | Data breaches and unauthorised access to sensitive information; expensive data recovery and security remediation; reputational damage; confidential information distributed with potential harm to clients, markets and firm; breaches of data protection laws; vulnerabilities exploited more easily |
| **Controls** | Robust firewall and intrusion detection systems; employee security awareness training; secure and resilient IT systems; strong access controls and authentication mechanisms; additional encryption and data protection measures |

## Resilience, crises and emergencies

A distinction the report draws sharply, and an easy one to get backwards:

| | Plan | Activated for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Crises** | Crisis Management Plan / Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP) | Catastrophe — including property destruction and/or loss of life; also significant events carrying heavy reputational risk, such as a greenwashing scandal |
| **Emergencies** | Business Continuity Plan (BCP) | Events that would otherwise stop regulated activity — **cyber assaults**, terrorist assault on or near offices, the next pandemic |

So a cyber attack sits under the **BCP**, not the DRP.

Wind-down: analysis uses scenarios where the Board and ExCo determine Foresight
Group LLP must cease regulated activity and surrender its Part 4A FSMA 2000
permissions. Wind-down would follow an inability to recover from an event or
series of events — for example a failure of controls, business continuity and
disaster recovery planning.

## Regulatory context

- In scope of the FCA's **Investment Firms Prudential Regime (IFPR)**, implemented
  through the **MIFIDPRU** rulebook, in force from 1 January 2022.
- **ICARA** (Internal Capital Adequacy and Risk Assessment) approved by the
  Executive Committee in October 2023.
- **Consumer Duty** effective 31 July 2023; implementation plan folded into
  Compliance's periodic reporting to the Compliance Officer, Risk Committee and
  Members Board.
- Financial crime framework on five pillars: **Governance, Risk Assessment, Due
  Diligence & KYC, Training & Awareness, Monitoring & Surveillance** — spanning all
  three lines, with the MLRO responsible for oversight. In addition to first-line
  quality assurance reviews, **second line reviews a sample of KYC files** via the
  compliance monitoring programme.

### A note on one answer

The question about who oversees the overall adequacy and effectiveness of the
Group-wide risk framework is ambiguous in the source. The report says the **Board**
is *accountable* for it, and three boxes later that the **Executive Committee** is
*responsible* for it — the same phrase attached to two bodies.

I answered Board, because the question asks who oversees and because the
delegation to the Executive Committee is tested separately. If the marking scheme
says Executive Committee, the report's wording is why.

The distinction underneath is worth more than the answer: **accountability can be
delegated in its execution and not in its consequence.** The Board remains
answerable for the framework regardless of who runs it day to day — which is the
same principle that makes a named risk owner meaningful on a register.
